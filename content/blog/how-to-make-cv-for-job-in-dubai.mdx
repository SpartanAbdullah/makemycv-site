To make a CV for a job in Dubai, start from the UAE conventions (one column, two pages at most, visa status near the top) and then tune eight things for Dubai's hiring machine: heavier ATS screening, recruiters who work over WhatsApp, and employers who think in ecosystems (DIFC, free zone, mainland, hospitality) rather than job titles alone. The eight steps below build the document in order, followed by a plain-text template you can copy and the Dubai-specific rules that separate a shortlisted CV from an ignored one. The base rules are in our [UAE CV guide](/blog/how-to-make-cv-for-job-in-uae); this page is the Dubai procedure.

## Step 1 — Write the Contact Block With Visa Status and Notice Period

The top five lines of a Dubai CV answer the recruiter's first practical question: *how complicated is it to hire this person?* Put these in the header, in this order:

- **Name and target job title** — the title as the posting words it, not an internal grade.
- **Phone with country code** — a UAE mobile (+971) if you have one; otherwise your home-country number with its code. Dubai agencies call and message, so the number must work on WhatsApp.
- **Email and city** — "Dubai, UAE" if you are here; your current city and country if you are applying from abroad.
- **Visa status** — employment visa, visit visa, Golden Visa, spouse-sponsored, or "relocating". Recruiters shortlist around this line.
- **Availability or notice period** — "Available immediately" on a visit visa is an advantage; say it plainly. If you are employed, state your notice period, and use the [notice period calculator](/notice-period-calculator) if you are unsure what your contract works out to.

Leave salary expectations out of the header, and out of the CV altogether; Step 7 and the rules section explain where they belong.

## Step 2 — Write a Three-Line Professional Summary Tuned to the Ecosystem

A Dubai summary does three jobs in three lines: it names the role and your years in it, it gives one provable result, and it names the kind of employer you are targeting. "Which Dubai" matters here, because a DIFC compliance team, a JAFZA trading company and a Jumeirah hotel read the same summary very differently (the four markets are listed under the Dubai-specific rules below).

Keep one master summary and shift it per application: a free-zone founder reading your CV wants clarity and commercial outcomes, a mainland corporate's ATS wants the posting's exact title and two or three of its phrases, and a DIFC firm wants the regulatory vocabulary you legitimately hold (DFSA, KYC/AML, IFRS). Skip "dynamic", "passionate" and "results-driven"; they carry no screening value and read as filler to a recruiter who screens CVs all day.

## Step 3 — Write Experience Bullets With AED Values and Scale

Dubai is a commercial market, and recruiters read experience bullets for scale: how much money, how many people, how many units, which region. Each bullet follows the same shape: verb, what you did, the scale, the outcome. The scale is where AED belongs, because a sales target, a budget, a portfolio value or a rent roll in dirhams tells a Dubai reader instantly what size of operation you ran.

Only use figures you can defend in an interview. Where you have no number, describe the scope in words (the team, the territory, the product line) rather than inventing one. If you use the AI bullet rewriter in [the builder](https://app.makemycv.ae), it will not invent a figure for you either: wherever a number belongs, it leaves a placeholder for you to fill in with the real value.

Pattern bullets, with the placeholders you replace:

- Grew [territory] sales from AED [amount] to AED [amount] in [number] months by [method].
- Managed a [number]-unit residential portfolio worth AED [amount] with [number]% average occupancy.
- Led a team of [number] across [number] outlets; cut staff turnover from [number]% to [number]%.
- Closed [number] KYC/AML remediation cases per month against a DFSA deadline with zero findings.

## Step 4 — List 8–12 Screenable Skills That Mirror the Posting

The skills section is where the ATS filter and the human screener both look first for exact matches, so it is built from the job posting, not from memory. Pull three or four exact phrases from the Dubai posting you are answering and use them verbatim; [JD Match](/jd-match) shows which of a posting's terms your CV already contains and which are missing. Keep the list to 8–12 hard skills, and move soft skills into the experience bullets where they can be shown rather than claimed.

Add the Dubai industry tokens you legitimately hold. Each sector has two or three that filters and screeners both look for: RERA certification for real estate, HACCP for F&B, DHA/MOH licensing for healthcare, Tasheel/GDRFA familiarity for PRO roles, and DFSA, KYC/AML, Basel and IFRS for DIFC finance and compliance. Including the ones you hold is the cheapest ranking improvement available; including ones you do not hold is the fastest way to fail the interview that follows.

## Step 5 — Education, With the Attestation Note

Education is one line per qualification: degree, institution, country, year. Dubai readers care about the country because it tells them how the qualification will be treated for visas and licensing, so never leave it out, and list the highest or most relevant qualification first.

If your degree has been attested for use in the UAE, say so on the same line. Some employers and the work-permit process ask for an attested degree, and one word on the CV removes a screening question before it is asked. If it has not been attested, do not claim it; leave the line as the plain qualification. Short professional certifications that carry weight in Dubai (RERA, HACCP, CFA, PMP, DHA licence) go in a separate certifications line so they are not buried under a decade-old degree.

## Step 6 — Languages, With Honest Levels

Dubai is multilingual and recruiters scan the languages line for a practical reason: who can this person serve, sell to and work alongside? List each language with a level the reader can rely on, for example "English — fluent", "Arabic — conversational", "Hindi — native". Arabic is an advantage in government-facing, retail and some client-facing roles, but it is not a requirement for most Dubai jobs, and stating "fluent" when you mean "basic" will be found out in the first call.

Put the languages the posting asks for first, and keep the line short. A language you cannot hold a work conversation in does not belong on a CV going to an employer who may test it.

## Step 7 — Add the UAE Essentials Western Templates Leave Out

Dubai recruiters expect a handful of fields that US and UK CV templates do not have, and their absence creates work for the reader. Four matter most:

- **Nationality** — expected on a UAE CV; it is routine information here, not a sensitive disclosure.
- **Driving licence** — for sales, logistics, real estate and field-service roles, "Valid UAE Driving Licence" is a screening keyword. It has a dedicated optional field in [the builder](https://app.makemycv.ae) for exactly this reason.
- **Photo** — sector-dependent. Hospitality and retail groups (Jumeirah, Address, Atlantis, the mall retailers) are photo-friendly and presentation-weighted; DIFC firms and mainland corporates treat it as optional. Make the choice deliberately rather than by template default.
- **Availability and visa status** — already in your header from Step 1; make sure the two agree with what you say in the application form.

Salary expectations are not a UAE essential. Dubai postings often ask for them in the application form, so answer there, where context is possible, and never on the CV document, where a number can only filter you out.

## Step 8 — Save as a Small PDF With a Chat-Safe Filename

Dubai recruiters and agencies routinely ask for your CV over WhatsApp, so the file itself is part of the application. Export one PDF, under a couple of MB, with a filename that works in a chat thread: `Firstname-Lastname-CV-2026.pdf`, not `final_v7 (3).pdf`. Your CV's first impression may literally be a chat bubble preview, and a sloppy filename reads as a sloppy candidate before the file is opened.

Keep the layout single-column with standard section headers so the ATS used by banks, airlines, telcos and DIFC firms parses it intact; tables, two-column designs and graphics frequently do not survive. Keep a DOCX copy only for the portals that ask for an editable file. ## Dubai-Specific Rules

These are the points where a Dubai CV differs from a generic UAE one. Apply them after the eight steps, not instead of them.

- **Know which Dubai you are applying to.** "Dubai" is at least four hiring markets wearing one skyline:
  - *DIFC (financial centre):* banks, funds, fintech, legal. English-first, heavily regulated, ATS-screened. Compliance vocabulary matters: DFSA, KYC/AML, Basel, IFRS. If you have it, your CV should say it.
  - *Free zones (JAFZA, DMCC, Dubai Internet/Media City, D3):* trading companies, logistics, tech, media. Often smaller HR teams, so your CV may be read by a founder rather than a recruiter. Clarity beats formality.
  - *Mainland corporates:* the giants, such as Emirates Group, Emaar, ENOC, Majid Al Futtaim, du and e&. High application volume and, at that scale, software screening before a human reads anything. This is where parse-safety decides outcomes before humans are involved.
  - *Hospitality and retail:* Jumeirah, Address, Atlantis, the malls' retail groups. Photo-friendly, presentation-weighted, often hiring at volume through agencies.
- **One CV, shifting summary and keywords.** The same document can serve all four markets; the professional summary and the skills line change per application.
- **Mirror the posting.** Three or four exact phrases from the job advert, used verbatim, is the cheapest ranking improvement available.
- **Pass the WhatsApp test.** One small PDF with a chat-safe filename, because the first view may be a chat bubble.
- **State logistics, not salary.** Visa status and notice period go on the CV; expected salary goes in the application form only.
- **Driving licence is a keyword** for sales, logistics, real estate and field roles.
- **Photo by sector**, not by template default.
