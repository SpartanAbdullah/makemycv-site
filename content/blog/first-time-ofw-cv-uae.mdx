If you're applying to Dubai, Abu Dhabi or the wider UAE as a first-time OFW,
your CV needs adapting — not because Philippine CVs are bad, but because the
two markets follow **different conventions**, and UAE recruiters (and their
ATS software) read against theirs. The changes are predictable: remove the
Philippine-specific blocks (government ID numbers, character references, the
signature line), add what UAE screening looks for (visa position,
availability, nationality), and translate your credentials — PRC licences,
CHED degrees, agency paperwork — into terms a Gulf recruiter recognises.
Here's the exact checklist.

## Why your Philippine CV reads differently in the UAE

Filipinos form one of the largest expatriate communities in the UAE, working
across hospitality, healthcare, retail, engineering, logistics and
administration — so recruiters in Dubai and Abu Dhabi see Philippine-format
CVs every day, and the ones adapted to the local market are easier to
shortlist. An
unadapted CV signals that the candidate hasn't researched UAE hiring, and
the format differences genuinely interfere with screening: personal-data
blocks eat the space recruiters scan first, character references push real
content to page three, and a missing visa line forces a follow-up question
many recruiters won't make.

## What to remove

These are standard on many Philippine CVs and out of place on a UAE CV:

- **SSS, TIN, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG numbers** — Philippine government
  identifiers that mean nothing to a UAE employer. A CV gets forwarded
  around; keep identifiers off it.
- **Character references** — "available upon request" is assumed, and named
  references belong at offer stage, not on the CV.
- **The signature line** — signing and dating a CV is a Philippine
  application convention that UAE CVs don't carry.
- **Religion and complete birth details** — not expected by UAE recruiters;
  date of birth is optional at most.
- **Full home address** — "Quezon City, Philippines" or "Al Barsha, Dubai"
  is enough.
- **Agency paperwork references** — your DMW processing, OEC (being
  phased into the digital OFW Pass) and OWWA documentation matter for
  deployment, but they are separate from the CV. Don't crowd the page with them.

## What to add

- **Visa position, stated plainly** — the single most UAE-specific line.
  From the Philippines: "Applying via DMW-licensed agency — available for
  deployment". Already in the UAE: "Visit visa — available immediately" or
  "Employment visa (transferable)". Recruiters screen on this early.
- **Nationality** — expected on UAE CVs, unlike Western markets.
- **Languages** — always. UAE recruiters screen for English —
  state your proficiency plainly, plus Filipino/Tagalog and any others; for
  many Filipino candidates this section is a genuine edge.
- **AED-quantified achievements** — convert peso figures in budgets, sales
  and savings to AED (or USD) so a Dubai recruiter absorbs them at a
  glance. Check the current rate and round honestly.
- **Attestation status** — the UAE is not part of the Apostille Convention,
  so the chain runs CHED CAV → DFA authentication → UAE Embassy legalisation
  in Manila → MOFAIC attestation in the UAE. If your degree has completed
  it, note it as attested; if not, start early — employers verify documents.
- **PRC licence, where you hold one** — with profession and validity.
  Healthcare roles additionally go through UAE licensing (DHA in Dubai,
  DOH in Abu Dhabi, MOHAP elsewhere); employers and agencies guide that
  process, but naming your PRC licence up front starts it.

## What to change

- **Length:** one to two pages. Keep depth for your most recent role;
  compress earlier ones to a line each.
- **Objective → summary:** replace "To obtain a position where I can grow"
  with two or three factual sentences tied to the role you're targeting —
  our [professional summary examples](/blog/professional-summary-examples-uae-cv)
  show the pattern by profession.
- **Photo:** optional but common in the UAE, especially in the
  client-facing sectors where many OFWs work — the opposite of US-style
  advice. Use a professional headshot in a dedicated header slot; our
  [photo guide](/blog/should-cv-have-photo-uae) has the sector-by-sector
  answer.
- **Duties → achievements:** "Assigned to handle guest concerns" becomes
  "Resolved 30+ guest concerns daily at a 250-room property, maintaining a
  9.1 review score". Numbers travel; duty lists don't.
- **File:** a text-based, single-column PDF — the format UAE applicant
  tracking systems parse most reliably.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a first-time OFW put on a CV for UAE jobs?

One to two pages, single column: a header with your nationality, current
location and visa position (in the Philippines and processing via agency, or
already in the UAE on a visit visa with availability), a short professional
summary, quantified work experience, education with attestation status,
skills, and languages. Leave out Philippine government ID numbers, character
references and the signature block — UAE recruiters don't use them.

### Should a Filipino CV for the UAE include a photo?

It's optional but common in the UAE — especially in hospitality, retail
and other client-facing sectors where many OFWs work. Use a
professional headshot in a dedicated header slot of an ATS-safe template,
never a cropped casual photo. This differs from US-style advice; the UAE
market accepts photos.

### Do I put my SSS, TIN or PhilHealth numbers on a UAE CV?

No. SSS, TIN, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG are Philippine government identifiers
with no meaning to a UAE employer, and a CV gets forwarded around — keep
personal identifiers off it. The same goes for your passport number: share
documents at offer and processing stage, not on the CV.

### How do I show my PRC licence or CHED degree on a UAE CV?

List the licence with its profession and validity (e.g. PRC-licensed Registered Nurse) and your degree with university, country and year. UAE employers verify attested documents, and the chain for the UAE runs CHED CAV, then DFA authentication, then UAE Embassy legalisation in Manila, then MOFAIC attestation in the UAE — the UAE is not part of the Apostille Convention, so an apostille alone is not enough. If your degree has completed that chain, note it as attested on the CV; if it is partway, attestation in progress is the honest line. Healthcare roles additionally go through UAE licensing (DHA in Dubai, DOH in Abu Dhabi, MOHAP elsewhere), which employers or agencies guide you through.

### Can I apply to UAE jobs directly or only through an agency?

For most first-time OFWs deploying from the Philippines, the standard route
is a DMW-licensed recruitment agency — direct hiring from abroad is
restricted, with limited exceptions. If you are already in the UAE on a
visit visa, you can apply to employers directly; state your visa status and
availability plainly on the CV, because UAE recruiters screen for it.
