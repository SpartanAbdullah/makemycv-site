The global narrative around hiring in 2026 is clear: employers care about skills, not degrees. Forbes reported in August 2026 that 70% of employers now use skills-based hiring, according to the NACE Job Outlook 2026 survey, and 53% have removed degree requirements entirely — up from 30%.

If you are a job seeker in the UAE reading that advice, the temptation is to de-emphasise your degree on your CV and lead entirely with skills and competencies. That is the advice most global career sites are giving right now.

In the Gulf, it is incomplete advice. And following it could cost you more than an interview.

## What the 2026 Data Actually Says

The numbers are real and come from credible sources.

The National Association of Colleges and Employers (NACE) Job Outlook 2026 survey found that **70% of employers now use skills-based hiring**, up from 65% a year earlier. The same survey found **53% have eliminated degree requirements**, roughly doubling from 30%. And in a spring 2026 update, NACE reported that **more than one-third of entry-level jobs now require some form of AI competency** — nearly triple the share from the previous autumn.

The direction is genuine. Employers, particularly in technology, are increasingly willing to hire based on demonstrated capability rather than a credential.

But there is a significant gap between what employers say and what employers do.

## The Catch: 45% Dropped Requirements in Name Only

Research from Harvard Business School and the Burning Glass Institute reviewed companies that publicly dropped degree requirements from their job postings. The finding was sobering: **45% did so "in name only."**

At some large firms, the change was virtually invisible in actual hiring — **fewer than 1 in 700 new hires were workers without bachelor's degrees**. The researchers found that the overall effect was modest: dropping requirements raised non-degree hires by 3.5 percentage points across the sample, or roughly 97,000 people out of 77 million annual hires.

About 37% of companies that made the change did achieve a meaningful increase — roughly 20% more non-degree workers. But the headline "employers are dropping degree requirements" overstates what is happening on the ground.

This is important context for any job seeker, anywhere. For a UAE job seeker, there is a second layer that the global advice misses entirely.

## Why the UAE Is Different: MoHRE Skill Levels 1 to 9

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation classifies every worker in the UAE into one of nine skill levels, aligned with International Labour Organisation standards. Your skill level is not a suggestion — it determines your work permit category.

| Skill level | Classification | Education requirement | Example roles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Managers | Bachelor's degree or specialised certification | Chief executives, production managers, hospitality managers |
| 2 | Professionals | Bachelor's degree or specialised certification | Engineers, health professionals, teachers, IT specialists |
| 3 | Technicians | Post-secondary diploma or high school certificate | Science associates, health associates, IT technicians |
| 4 | Clerical | Post-secondary diploma or high school certificate | General clerks, customer service, recording clerks |
| 5 | Service and sales | Post-secondary diploma or high school certificate | Personal service, sales workers, protective services |
| 6–9 | Operational and elementary | No formal degree requirement | Agricultural, trades, operators, labourers |

The practical consequence: **a candidate classified at levels 1 or 2 has a different visa category from a candidate at levels 6 to 9.** That difference affects sponsorship eligibility, the ability to bring dependants, and in some cases the duration and conditions of the residence visa.

When a US career coach says "your degree doesn't matter, your skills do," they are speaking in a market where a degree has no regulatory function beyond signalling. In the UAE, your degree has a regulatory function. It gates your permit category.

## Degree Attestation: The Step That Still Gates Your Visa

Beyond classification, most UAE employment visas for skilled roles require an **attested degree certificate** before the work permit is issued. Attestation is a multi-step chain: your university confirms the degree, your home country's foreign affairs ministry stamps it, the UAE Embassy in your country verifies it, and the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs provides final attestation.

This process takes weeks and sometimes months. It is not optional for roles at skill levels 1 to 3. And it means your degree is not just a persuasion tool on your CV — it is a document the employer's PRO will need to physically submit to the government.

A CV that buries or omits the degree creates practical friction: the recruiter or HR team has to ask for it, which adds a step, which in a competitive market can mean someone else's application moves faster.

## How to Structure a Gulf CV That Leads With Skills Without Hiding Qualifications

The solution is not to choose between skills and qualifications. It is to give each one the right job on your CV.

**Skills carry the persuasion.** Your professional summary, your achievement bullets, and your skills section are where you demonstrate what you can do and what impact you have had. This is where the skills-based hiring movement is genuinely relevant — recruiters and ATS software are looking for specific capabilities, not degree names.

**Qualifications carry the credibility and the regulatory signal.** Your Education section — placed after Work Experience, not before — lists your degree, the institution, the year, and any relevant certifications. It is there because the employer needs it for the work permit process, and because removing it creates a gap that experienced UAE recruiters will notice. For the [correct section order and formatting](/blog/cv-format-uae-2026), the full structure guide covers it in detail.

**A practical structure:**

1. Professional title and contact details (including visa status — see [why this field matters](/blog/expat-cv-uae-guide))
2. Professional summary — three to four lines leading with your strongest skills and a quantified achievement
3. Work experience — reverse-chronological, achievement-led, with measurable results
4. Skills section — specific, verifiable, aligned to the target role
5. Education — degree, institution, year, plus professional certifications
6. Languages and additional certifications

The degree is present. It is not the lead. The skills and achievements are doing the persuasive work. The degree is doing the regulatory work. Both are necessary in a Gulf CV.

## If You Do Not Have a Degree: What to Put in That Space

Not having a degree does not make you unemployable in the UAE — it changes which roles and visa categories are available to you.

**What to include:**

- Your highest completed qualification, whatever it is — a diploma, a professional certification, a trade qualification.
- Industry-recognised certifications that are relevant to your target role. In some fields — IT, project management, certain trades — professional certifications carry significant weight and can support a case for a higher skill-level classification.
- Measurable achievements in your work experience. In the absence of a degree, your track record becomes the entire argument. Quantify everything you can.

**What to know:**

- Without a degree, you will typically be classified at MoHRE skill levels 6 to 9, which limits your visa type. Some employers can argue for a higher classification based on extensive experience and professional certifications, but this is employer-dependent, not guaranteed.
- The roles most genuinely open to non-degree candidates in the UAE are in trades, operations, hospitality, retail, and certain technology roles where portfolio and certifications substitute for formal education. ## The Honest Take

The skills-over-degrees movement is real, directionally correct, and gaining momentum. It is also oversold in the global press and actively misleading for Gulf job seekers who follow it without understanding the local regulatory context.

In the UAE, your degree is not just a credential. It is a document that determines your work permit category, gates your visa process, and affects whether you can sponsor your family. Removing it from your CV because a Forbes article told you skills matter more is optimising for a filter that does not govern your market.

Lead with skills. Keep your qualifications visible. That is the Gulf answer to a global question.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do you need a degree to get a job in the UAE?

It depends on the role and the visa category. MoHRE classifies workers into skill levels 1 through 9, and levels 1 and 2 — which include managers and professionals — require a bachelor's degree or specialised certification. Levels 3 to 5 require post-secondary diplomas or high school certificates. Working without a degree is possible at levels 6 to 9, but those categories limit your visa type and your ability to sponsor dependants.

### Have employers really dropped degree requirements?

Partly. The NACE Job Outlook 2026 survey found that 53% of employers have eliminated degree requirements, up from 30%. But research from Harvard Business School and the Burning Glass Institute found that 45% of companies that publicly dropped requirements did so in name only — fewer than 1 in 700 hires at some large firms were actually non-graduates.

### What are MoHRE skill levels 1 to 9?

MoHRE classifies all UAE workers into nine skill levels based on education and job complexity, aligned with International Labour Organisation standards. Levels 1 and 2 are highly skilled roles requiring a bachelor's degree. Levels 3 to 5 are semi-skilled roles requiring diplomas or high school certificates. Levels 6 to 9 are operational roles with no formal degree requirement. Your skill level affects your work permit category and visa conditions.

### What is degree attestation for a UAE employment visa?

Degree attestation is the process of having your university certificate verified and stamped by a chain of authorities — typically your university, your home country's foreign affairs ministry, the UAE Embassy in your country, and the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Most UAE employment visas for skilled roles require an attested degree before the work permit is issued.

### Should I remove my degree from my CV to look more skills-focused?

No. In the UAE, your degree serves a regulatory function beyond persuasion — it determines your MoHRE skill level, your work permit category, and your eligibility to sponsor dependants. Lead your CV with skills and achievements, but keep qualifications in a clearly labelled Education section. Removing them creates a gap that delays your hiring process.

### What should I put on a UAE CV if I do not have a degree?

List your highest completed qualification, relevant professional certifications, and any industry training. Emphasise measurable achievements and specific skills in your work experience. Be aware that without a degree you will be classified at MoHRE skill levels 6 to 9, which limits visa type — professional certifications can sometimes support a case for a higher classification, depending on the role and the employer.
