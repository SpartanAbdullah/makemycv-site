Most UAE CVs that go nowhere are not rejected for weak experience. They are **filtered out for mechanical reasons** before a recruiter reads the second paragraph: a missing visa status line, a two-column template that an applicant tracking system (ATS) reads out of order, a passport number where a professional summary should be, or a one-page US-style document with the UAE fields squeezed off. The fifteen mistakes below are the ones that do the damage in Dubai and Abu Dhabi specifically, each with the reason it hurts here and a one-line fix. Short on time? Fix numbers 1, 3, 11 and 13, then run the result through the free [ATS resume checker](/resume-checker).

This is the long version of the six-item list in our [UAE CV format guide](/blog/cv-format-uae-2026); that guide covers section order, this one covers what goes wrong with the document itself. If the CV is sound and you are still hearing nothing, the cause is usually the market rather than the file: see [why your CV is being ignored in Dubai](/blog/why-cv-ignored-dubai).

## 1. No visa status line

UAE work visas are employer-sponsored, so the first thing a recruiter needs to know is what hiring you involves: a transfer, a new visa, or nothing. A CV that leaves it out forces a guess, and the guess is usually "needs sponsorship, will take time". Stating the status matters more than the status itself; candidates on visit and cancelled visas are shortlisted every day because the recruiter can plan around a known position.

**Fix:** one header line: *Employment visa, transferable* / *Visit visa, available immediately* / *Cancelled visa, available immediately* / *Applying from [country], relocating on offer*. Our [expat CV guide](/blog/expat-cv-uae-guide) has the wording for each case.

## 2. Forcing everything onto a one-page Western template

The one-page rule is a US convention, and the templates that enforce it were designed for a market with no visa field, no nationality field and no languages block. Compressing a mid-career UAE CV onto one page nearly always removes exactly those lines, plus the certifications that regulated sectors here check first. The vocabulary question is covered in [CV vs resume in the UAE](/blog/cv-vs-resume-uae).

**Fix:** one page for under five years of experience, two pages for everyone else, and never cut a UAE essential to save a line.

## 3. A two-column or sidebar template

Two-column layouts look designed and parse badly. Most large UAE employers run CVs through an ATS before a person sees them, and the parser reads left to right across both columns, so sidebar skills land inside job titles and dates detach from employers. The recruiter sees a garbled text record, not your design. [Does Dubai use ATS?](/blog/does-dubai-use-ats) explains which employers filter this way.

**Fix:** single column, standard headings (Work Experience, Education, Skills, Languages, Certifications), no text boxes.

## 4. A photo pasted in as a header image

A headshot is optional and common in UAE client-facing sectors, so the photo itself is rarely the problem. The problem is how it goes in: floated inside the text column, or a whole header exported as one image with your name and contact details baked into it, so the parser loses all three. Sector-by-sector guidance is in [should your CV have a photo in the UAE](/blog/should-cv-have-photo-uae).

**Fix:** if your sector expects a photo, use a template with a dedicated photo slot and keep your name and contact details as real text.

## 5. Emirates ID, passport number and a full identity block

Many CVs arriving from South Asia, the Philippines and parts of Africa open with passport number, Emirates ID, date of birth, marital status, religion and father's name, because home-country application forms demand them. None of it helps a UAE recruiter shortlist you, and identity numbers on a document forwarded between agencies and hiring managers are a data-protection liability the employer did not ask for; the UAE's personal data protection law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) is one reason HR teams collect these details on their own onboarding forms.

**Fix:** keep nationality, current emirate and visa status; delete every document number; treat date of birth as optional.

## 6. "References available on request"

This line was a convention when CVs were posted. Today it tells the reader nothing they did not assume and occupies the last line on the page, which a tight two-page CV can least afford. UAE employers run reference and background checks at offer stage through their own process; nobody reads a CV looking for permission to ask.

**Fix:** delete the line. If a job ad asks for referees, supply them in the application form.

## 7. Salary history on the CV

UAE job ads and application forms routinely ask for current and expected salary, which leads candidates to pre-empt the question on the CV itself. That is the wrong document. A salary line anchors the negotiation before anyone has read your experience, and for candidates arriving from lower-paying markets it invites an offer benchmarked to your old pay rather than the UAE role.

**Fix:** leave salary off the CV; answer the expected-salary question in the form or the screening call, in AED, as a range.

## 8. Unexplained gaps

Gaps are more common in the UAE than in most markets because a visa cancellation, a relocation or a dependent-visa period produces one, and recruiters know this. What they do not tolerate is a gap with no explanation, because an unexplained twelve months reads as something being hidden. A one-line reason turns it from a question into a fact.

**Fix:** add a dated line in the timeline, such as *Jan to Aug 2025: relocated to the UAE on a family visa; completed PMP*. Our guide to [employment gaps on a UAE CV](/blog/employment-gaps-uae-cv) shows three ways to do it.

## 9. No notice period or availability

UAE Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021) allows contractual notice of 30 to 90 days, and employers hiring in the UAE need to know where you sit in that range before they progress you. A CV with no availability line pushes the question to a phone call that may never come, especially when another candidate has written *available immediately* in the header.

**Fix:** one line beside your visa status: *30-day notice* or *available immediately*. Not sure what your contract requires? The [notice period calculator](/notice-period-calculator) works it out.

## 10. Mixed or ambiguous date formats

*Jan 2022*, *01/2022*, *2022* and *January '22* in the same document look careless to a human and confuse a parser building a timeline. Numeric dates add a second problem in the UAE, where candidates from day-first and month-first countries sit in the same shortlist: *03/05/2024* means two different dates to two readers.

**Fix:** one format, month name and year, throughout: *Jan 2022 to Mar 2024*. Our [ATS CV checklist](/blog/ats-cv-checklist-uae) covers the other formatting rules.

## 11. Text saved as an image

A CV built in a design tool and exported as a flattened image, a phone photograph of a printed CV, or a scanned PDF contains no selectable text. To an ATS it is a blank page; to a recruiter searching a database for "Opera PMS" or "IFRS", you do not exist. It is the most damaging mistake on the list because it is invisible to the person who made it: the PDF looks perfect on screen.

**Fix:** export a text-based PDF from a word processor or CV builder, then test it: if you cannot highlight and copy a sentence from the PDF, neither can the ATS.

## 12. Hobbies, soft-skill lists and other padding

"Reading, travelling, cricket" and a bulleted list of *hardworking, team player, quick learner* add nothing a UAE recruiter can verify, and they usually appear on CVs short of measurable results, so padding reads as a signal that the substance ran out. The exception is a hobby that proves a relevant skill, such as a marathon finisher applying to a fitness brand.

**Fix:** replace the soft-skills list with evidence inside your experience bullets, and drop hobbies unless one directly supports the role. The [UAE CV examples page](/cv-examples-uae) shows weak-versus-strong bullets by sector.

## 13. A generic objective statement

*Seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation where I can utilise my skills* is one of the most common opening lines on CVs arriving in the UAE and it has no content. Recruiters read the first three lines to decide whether to read the rest, and an objective statement spends those lines saying nothing about the role you are applying for.

**Fix:** a two- or three-sentence professional summary naming your target role, years of experience, sector and one measurable result, in the vocabulary of the job ad. Templates are in [professional summary examples for UAE CVs](/blog/professional-summary-examples-uae-cv).

## 14. Keyword stuffing

Knowing that ATS software scores keyword matches, some candidates paste the whole job description into the skills section, repeat *project management* nine times, or hide keywords in white text. Parsers flag the white-text trick, repetition adds nothing after the first occurrence, and a human reader sees a skills section that lists fifty items and proves none. In a market as competitive as Dubai, the recruiter has another CV to move on to.

**Fix:** mirror the job ad's exact terms once each, inside bullets that show you using them. Paste the ad into [JD Match](/jd-match) to see which required terms your CV genuinely covers.

## 15. No location or emirate

Recruiters filter on location. A CV with no city tells a Dubai hiring manager nothing about whether you are a ten-minute drive away, commuting from Abu Dhabi or applying from overseas, and each changes the hiring plan. A phone number without a country code has the same effect: it cannot be dialled from a recruiter's system and signals a CV written for another market.

**Fix:** *Dubai, UAE* (or the city you are applying from), a *+971* number written in full, and a professional email address, all on the first two lines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my CV getting rejected in the UAE without any interview?

Usually for mechanical reasons, not experience. The common ones are a missing visa status line, a two-column template that an ATS reads out of order, a scanned or image-based PDF with no selectable text, and a summary that does not use the job ad's own words. Fix those four first. If the CV is sound and you still hear nothing, the problem is more likely the roles you are applying to than the document.

### Should I put my Emirates ID or passport number on my CV?

No. Identity document numbers belong on the onboarding paperwork the employer sends you at offer stage, not on a document that is forwarded between recruiters, agencies and hiring managers. UAE recruiters do not need either number to shortlist you, and the UAE's personal data protection law gives employers reasons to prefer not to receive it unasked. Keep nationality and visa status; remove the numbers.

### Do I have to include my salary on a UAE CV?

No. UAE job ads frequently ask for current and expected salary, but the right place for that is the application form or the recruiter's screening call, where the number can be framed. Salary history on the CV anchors negotiation before anyone has read your experience and, for candidates arriving from lower-paying markets, invites an offer benchmarked to your old pay rather than the UAE role.

### Is a one-page CV too short for UAE jobs?

For a fresh graduate or someone with under five years of experience, one page is fine. For anyone else, forcing the CV onto one page usually strips out the UAE-specific lines recruiters filter on: visa status, nationality, languages, availability and certifications. UAE convention is one to two pages. Keep the second page if that is what it costs to keep those fields.

### Should I write my notice period on my CV in the UAE?

Yes, in the header or the personal details block, as one line: 'Available immediately', '30-day notice' or '60-day notice, negotiable'. UAE Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021) allows contractual notice between 30 and 90 days, so recruiters need the number to plan a start date. Leaving it out does not hide a long notice period; it moves the question to a phone call that may not happen.
