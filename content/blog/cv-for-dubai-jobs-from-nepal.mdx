If you're applying from Kathmandu, Pokhara, Biratnagar or Butwal to jobs in
Dubai and the wider UAE, your CV needs adapting — not because Nepali CVs are
wrong, but because Gulf recruiters (and their ATS software) read against
**different conventions**. The changes are predictable: state your visa
position and nationality up front, convert figures into AED, cut the personal
details the UAE doesn't use, and know how your degree gets recognised. Here's
the exact checklist — plus the three things specific to this route: converting
Bikram Sambat dates, labour approval, and the legal cap on what a manpower
agency may charge you.

## Why the same CV reads differently in Dubai than in Kathmandu

The UAE absorbs a very large share of Nepali labour migration. In Mangsir 2081
(mid-November to mid-December 2024), 24,769 Nepalis received labour permits for
the UAE — ahead of Saudi Arabia (18,115), Qatar (14,844) and Malaysia (4,469),
according to Department of Foreign Employment figures
[reported by My Republica](https://ceslam.org/news-media/uae-emerges-top-destination-for-nepali-migrant-workers/)
in December 2024. One caveat worth carrying: that count combines *new and
renewed* permits, so it partly reflects the size of the Nepali workforce
already in the UAE rather than fresh departures alone.

Either way, the practical consequence holds. Dubai recruiters see Nepali CVs
constantly, so the ones adapted to Gulf conventions are easier to shortlist —
and the unadapted ones lose on mechanics rather than merit. A missing visa line
forces a follow-up question most recruiters won't make, rupee figures force a
currency conversion mid-scan, a Bikram Sambat graduation year reads as a typo,
and a citizenship number sitting where a professional summary should be tells a
recruiter the CV was written for a different market.

## What to remove

- **Citizenship number and passport number** — share documents during
  processing, never on a CV that gets forwarded around.
- **Father's or husband's name** — a standard Nepali and wider South Asian
  convention, and not a UAE one.
- **Marital status, religion, caste and date of birth** — home-market
  conventions UAE recruiters don't expect.
- **SEE, SLC and +2 mark sheets** — once you hold a diploma or degree,
  school-leaving results and percentage tables don't belong. Mark-sheet tables
  also parse badly in ATS software.
- **Full home address** — "Kathmandu, Nepal" is enough, or "Al Qusais, Dubai"
  if you're already in the UAE.
- **Referees and their phone numbers** — references belong at offer stage.
- **The declaration block** — if your CV ends with "I hereby declare that the
  above information is true" plus a signature and date, delete it. Our
  [Indian CV adaptation guide](/blog/adapt-indian-cv-for-uae-jobs) covers why
  this convention travels badly.

## What to add

- **Visa position, stated plainly** — the single most Gulf-specific line.
  From Nepal: "Applying from Kathmandu — available to relocate on offer".
  Already in the UAE: "Employment visa (transferable)" or "Visit visa —
  available immediately". UAE work visas are employer-sponsored, so recruiters
  read this line first to understand the logistics.
- **Nationality** — expected on Gulf CVs, unlike many Western markets.
- **AED-converted achievements** — "handled daily sales of NPR 4 lakh" makes a
  recruiter stop and convert; "handled daily sales of ≈AED 11,000" doesn't.
  Check the current rate and round honestly. The Nepali rupee is pegged to the
  Indian rupee, so if you've worked with INR figures, convert those too.
- **Attestation status** — see the section below; one line saves an email.
- **Languages, listed properly** — Nepali, Hindi and English is a genuinely
  strong combination in UAE hospitality, retail, security and facilities
  management, where teams and customers span South Asia. State each with an
  honest level rather than listing them bare.
- **Named systems and standards** — this is where Nepali experience translates
  strongest. If you've worked to specific safety standards, on named property
  management or POS systems, with airline ground-handling procedures, or under
  a recognisable hotel brand's operating standards, name them. Gulf recruiters
  recognise systems and standards even when they don't know your employer.

## What to change

Two of these are specific to Nepali documents, and they're the ones that most
often cause silent damage:

- **Dates: convert Bikram Sambat to Gregorian.** Your certificates, experience
  letters and often your CV carry BS years — 2076, 2081 — and Nepal's calendar
  runs roughly 56 years and 8 months ahead. A Gulf recruiter reading "2081"
  sees a typo, and an ATS parsing it either discards the date or files your
  degree in the future. Write AD years on the CV (2019, 2024). Where a scanned
  certificate shows BS, give both: "2076 BS (2019)".
- **Name: match your passport exactly.** Devanagari names get transliterated
  inconsistently across school certificates, experience letters and the
  passport itself — Shrestha/Shreshtha, Bikram/Vikram. The CV should carry the
  passport spelling, because that's the one every downstream document will be
  checked against. If your certificates use a different spelling, keep it out
  of the CV and raise it at document stage rather than letting a recruiter
  discover the mismatch.
- **Length:** one to two pages. Depth for your latest role; a line each for
  older ones.
- **Objective → summary:** replace the generic objective with two or three
  factual sentences tied to the target role — see our
  [professional summary examples](/blog/professional-summary-examples-uae-cv).
- **Photo:** optional but common in the UAE, especially in client-facing
  sectors. Use a professional headshot in a dedicated header slot, or skip it;
  our [photo guide](/blog/should-cv-have-photo-uae) has the sector table.
- **Duties → achievements:** "Responsible for store operations" becomes
  "Ran a 9-staff store; cut stock discrepancies 30% in six months". Numbers
  travel; duty lists don't.
- **File:** a text-based, single-column PDF — what UAE applicant tracking
  systems parse most reliably.

## Labour approval: what it is, and when it actually matters

Under Nepal's **Foreign Employment Act 2064 (2007)**, any Nepali citizen
leaving for foreign employment must hold labour approval — *Shram Swikriti* —
from the Department of Foreign Employment before departure. India is the only
exception. It's issued through the DoFE's FEIMS system, and it's what puts you
inside the Foreign Employment Welfare Fund and the insurance cover attached to
it. Leaving without it means leaving both behind.

Two things about it are worth getting straight, because candidates on this
route routinely get them backwards:

- **It comes after the offer, not before.** Labour approval is processed once
  you have a job and a visa in hand. It is not a prerequisite for applying, and
  not something you need before sending a CV.
- **It doesn't belong on the CV.** No UAE recruiter is looking for it in your
  document. Where it matters is the moment someone asks how soon you could
  join — that's when knowing your own timeline, including approval and a
  medical fitness test at an approved centre, makes you sound like a candidate
  who has done this properly.

If you're already in the UAE and renewing, the same system handles re-entry
and renewal approvals. On the CV, that history is simply continuous UAE
experience — which is worth more to a Gulf recruiter than anything you did
before it.

## Getting your degree recognised: legalisation, not apostille

**Neither Nepal nor the UAE is a party to the Hague Apostille Convention**
(per the HCCH status table for the 1961 Convention). An apostille therefore has
no standing on this route, and any agent offering you one for UAE use has
misunderstood the requirement or is selling you something you can't use.

The chain runs:

1. Verification by the issuing university or board, and Nepal's **Ministry of
   Education**
2. **Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Nepal**
3. **UAE Embassy in Kathmandu**
4. **MOFAIC** attestation inside the UAE

Employers typically ask at offer stage, and the chain takes time, so start
early rather than at the point someone's waiting on it. On the CV, one line
does the job: *BBA, Tribhuvan University, 2019 — attested*, or *attestation in
progress*.

## What this should cost you — and what it shouldn't

This is the part worth knowing before you talk to any agency.

Nepal's **"free visa, free ticket"** directive has been in force since **July
2015**. It requires the *foreign employer* to bear the visa and air ticket
costs for Nepali workers going to seven destinations — the UAE, Saudi Arabia,
Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman and Malaysia — and it caps what a licensed
recruitment agency may charge the worker at **NPR 10,000**.

The honest caveat: the cap is widely broken. Reporting by
[FairSquare](https://fairsq.org/press-release-nepal-should-eliminate-recruitment-fees-and-increase-enforcement-capacity-to-prevent-exploitation-of-its-migrant-workers/)
and [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2024/12/the-hidden-costs-of-nepals-free-visa-free-ticket-policy/)
documents workers paying many multiples of the legal figure. Nepal's own
**Office of the Auditor General** has found the policy's promise largely
unfulfilled, noting that the Department of Foreign Employment had not monitored
whether employers or recruiting agencies actually paid the airfare, insurance
and visa costs the directive assigns to them. Knowing the legal number doesn't
make the pressure disappear — but it does tell you exactly what you're being
asked to accept.

Alongside it, the fraud tells that hold across every Gulf route:

- **Legitimate employers and licensed agencies never charge you for a job
  offer, a work visa, or access to an interview.** UAE work permits are
  employer-sponsored and employer-paid.
- **Treat an offer that arrives before any interview as a scam.**
- **Verify the company independently** — find the employer's official website
  and confirm the vacancy through it, not through the person who approached
  you.
- **Check the agency is licensed** with the Department of Foreign Employment
  before you hand over documents or money.
- Get everything in writing, and keep your original certificates.

The costs you do legitimately carry are worth naming, because a vague "fees"
line is exactly where inflated charges hide: your passport, document
attestation, the medical fitness test, the Foreign Employment Welfare Fund
contribution, the mandatory insurance premium, and pre-departure orientation.
Those are paid to government offices and approved centres, and they have set
amounts you can check. Anything beyond them — and beyond the NPR 10,000 service
charge — is not a cost, it's a markup. Ask for a written breakdown and a
receipt for each line.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I write a CV for Dubai jobs from Nepal?

One to two pages, single column, saved as a text-based PDF. Near the top: your
nationality, current city, a reachable number with country code (+977), and
your visa position stated honestly — applying from Nepal, or already in the UAE
with your availability. Then a short factual summary, quantified work
experience with figures converted to AED, education with attestation status,
skills and languages. Leave out citizenship number, father's name, marital
status and SEE or SLC marks once you hold a higher qualification.

### Do I need labour approval (Shram Swikriti) to work in Dubai?

Yes. Under Nepal's Foreign Employment Act 2064 (2007), any Nepali citizen
leaving for foreign employment must obtain labour approval from the Department
of Foreign Employment before departure — India is the only exception. It is
issued through the DoFE's FEIMS system, and it comes after a job offer, not
before, so it does not block you from applying. Leaving without it puts you
outside the Foreign Employment Welfare Fund and the insurance cover attached to
it. The CV itself never mentions Shram Swikriti; it matters when a recruiter
asks how quickly you can join.

### How do I get my Nepali degree attested for the UAE?

Through a legalisation chain, not an apostille. Neither Nepal nor the UAE is a
party to the Hague Apostille Convention, so an apostille has no standing on
this route. The sequence runs: verification of the certificate by the issuing
university or board and Nepal's Ministry of Education, then Nepal's Ministry of
Foreign Affairs, then the UAE Embassy in Kathmandu, then MOFAIC attestation
inside the UAE. Employers usually ask at offer stage. Noting on your CV that
your degree is attested, or that attestation is in progress, pre-empts the
question.

### How much should a manpower agency charge for a Dubai job?

Very little. Nepal's "free visa, free ticket" directive, in force since July
2015, requires the foreign employer to pay the visa and air ticket costs for
Nepali workers going to seven destinations including the UAE, and caps what a
licensed recruitment agency may charge the worker at NPR 10,000. Reporting by
FairSquare and The Diplomat shows the cap is widely broken in practice, with
workers paying many times that figure. Knowing the legal number is your
protection: if an agency asks for NPR 100,000 to place you in Dubai, that is a
breach of the directive, not a market rate.

### Should I put my SEE or SLC results on a UAE CV?

Not once you hold a +2, diploma, bachelor's or higher — list your highest
qualifications with institution, country and year instead. School-leaving
results earn their place only when they are your top credential. The same
applies to long training-certificate lists: keep the ones that match the role
you are targeting and drop the rest, because they push your actual work
experience further down the page.

### Do I need a UAE visa before applying for jobs from Nepal?

No — UAE work visas are employer-sponsored, so the normal sequence is offer
first, then the employer processes your work permit and residence visa, and you
complete Nepal's labour approval before flying. What matters on the CV is
stating your position plainly: applying from Nepal and available to relocate,
or already in the UAE with your availability. Treat anyone selling you a UAE
work visa or a guaranteed job as a fraud — legitimate employers and licensed
