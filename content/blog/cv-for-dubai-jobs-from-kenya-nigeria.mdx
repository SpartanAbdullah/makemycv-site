If you're applying from Nairobi, Lagos, Abuja or Mombasa to jobs in Dubai
and the wider Gulf, your CV needs adapting — not because Kenyan or Nigerian
CVs are wrong, but because Gulf recruiters (and their ATS software) read
against **different conventions**. The changes are predictable: state your
visa position and nationality up front, convert achievements into AED,
trim secondary-school detail, and know how your degree gets recognised in
the UAE. Here's the exact checklist — plus the fraud warnings that matter
on this route.

## Why your CV reads differently in the Gulf

East and West African professional communities in the UAE are large and
growing — in logistics, hospitality, banking, fintech, healthcare,
security and aviation — so Dubai recruiters see Kenyan and Nigerian CVs
regularly, and the ones adapted to Gulf conventions are easier to
shortlist. An unadapted CV loses on mechanics: a missing visa line forces a follow-up
question most recruiters won't make, naira and shilling figures force a
currency conversion mid-scan, and long certificate appendixes push your
real experience below the fold.

## What to remove

- **WAEC, NECO and KCSE result details** — once you hold a degree, HND or
  diploma, secondary-school results don't belong. List your highest
  qualifications only.
- **Full home address** — "Lagos, Nigeria" or "Nairobi, Kenya" is enough;
  "Al Nahda, Dubai" if you're already in the UAE.
- **Religion, marital status, state of origin, local government area** —
  home-market conventions that UAE recruiters don't expect.
- **Referees and their phone numbers** — references belong at offer stage,
  not on a CV that gets forwarded around.
- **Passport and ID numbers** — share documents during processing, never
  on the CV itself.
- **NYSC administrative detail** (Nigeria) — your discharge status can be
  one line if the role or employer values it; the certificate itself is a
  home-market document, not a UAE screening item.

## What to add

- **Visa position, stated plainly** — the single most Gulf-specific line.
  From home: "Applying from Nairobi — available to relocate on offer".
  Already in the UAE: "Visit visa — available immediately" or "Employment
  visa (transferable)". UAE work visas are employer-sponsored, so
  recruiters read this line first to understand the logistics.
- **Nationality** — expected on Gulf CVs, unlike many Western markets.
- **AED-converted achievements** — "grew branch deposits by KSh 40M"
  or "managed a ₦300M portfolio" reads instantly when converted:
  "grew branch deposits by ≈AED 1.1M". Check the current rate and round
  honestly; USD works for global roles.
- **Attestation status** — the UAE is not part of the Apostille
  Convention, so the chain runs: authentication in your home country →
  UAE Embassy legalisation there → MOFAIC attestation in the UAE. The UAE
  embassies in Abuja and Nairobi now bundle the embassy and MOFAIC steps
  into one digital application, so the chain can be completed before you
  travel. If your degree has completed it, note it as attested; if not,
  start early — employers ask at offer stage.
- **Named platforms and regulators** — this is where Kenyan and Nigerian
  experience translates strongly. If you worked with M-Pesa rails, agency
  banking, mobile lending, CBN or CBK compliance regimes, name them: Gulf
  banking and fintech recruiters recognise platform experience even when
  they don't know your employer.

## What to change

- **Length:** one to two pages. Depth for your latest role; a line each
  for older ones.
- **Objective → summary:** replace the generic objective with two or three
  factual sentences tied to the target role — see our
  [professional summary examples](/blog/professional-summary-examples-uae-cv).
- **Photo:** optional but common in the UAE, especially client-facing
  sectors — different from the no-photo rule many Kenyan and Nigerian
  applicants follow for Western employers. Use a professional headshot in
  a dedicated header slot, or skip it; our
  [photo guide](/blog/should-cv-have-photo-uae) has the sector table.
- **Duties → achievements:** "Responsible for branch operations" becomes
  "Ran a 14-staff branch; grew active accounts 22% in a year". Numbers
  travel; duty lists don't.
- **File:** a text-based, single-column PDF — what UAE applicant tracking
  systems parse most reliably.

## The fraud check — read this before any agency

Fake-agency and fake-offer fraud targeting African jobseekers heading to
the Gulf is real, and the tells are consistent:

- **Legitimate employers and licensed agencies never charge you for a job
  offer, a work visa, or access to an interview.** UAE work permits are
  employer-sponsored and employer-paid.
- **Treat an offer that arrives before any interview as a scam.** The
  same goes for an interviewer who asks for payment to "reserve" the
  role.
- **Verify the company independently** — find the employer's official
  website and confirm the vacancy or the recruiter through it, not through
  the contact who approached you.
- Get everything in writing, and keep your originals until you've verified
  who you're dealing with.

A real recruiter's questions are about your experience. The moment they
ask you to pay them — for the offer, the visa, or the interview itself —
walk away. The costs you do carry yourself, like attestation, medical
tests and a police clearance certificate, are paid to government offices
and approved centres — never to the person offering you the job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I write a CV for Dubai jobs from Kenya or Nigeria?

One to two pages, single column, saved as a text-based PDF. Near the top:
your nationality, current city, a reachable number with country code (+254
or +234), and your visa position stated honestly — applying from home, or
in the UAE on a visit visa with availability. Then a short factual summary,
quantified work experience with figures converted to AED, education with
attestation status, skills and languages. Leave out secondary-school result
details once you hold a degree or diploma.

### Do I need a UAE visa before applying for Dubai jobs?

No — UAE work visas are employer-sponsored, so the normal sequence is offer
first, then the employer processes your work permit and residence visa.
Many candidates also job-hunt from inside the UAE on a visit visa; if that
is your situation, say so on the CV with your availability. Be wary of
anyone who offers to sell you a work visa or a guaranteed job — legitimate
employers and licensed agencies do not charge candidates for offers.

### Should my CV include WAEC or KCSE results?

Not once you hold a degree, HND or diploma — list your highest
qualifications with institution, country and year instead.
Secondary-school results earn their place only when they are your highest
credential. The same goes for long certificate lists: keep the ones
relevant to the role you are targeting.

### How does my Nigerian or Kenyan degree get recognised in the UAE?

Through document attestation. The chain runs: authentication in your home
country, then legalisation by the UAE Embassy there, then Ministry of
Foreign Affairs (MOFAIC) attestation inside the UAE — the UAE is not part
of the Apostille Convention, so an apostille alone is not enough. Employers
typically ask for attested certificates at offer stage; noting that your
degree is attested, or that attestation is in progress, pre-empts the
question.

### Is it safe to use a recruitment agency for Dubai jobs?

Licensed agencies are a normal part of Gulf hiring, but fake-agency fraud
targeting African jobseekers is real. The reliable tells: legitimate
recruiters never charge you for a job offer or a work visa, never ask you
to pay to unlock an interview, and make offers in writing from a verifiable
company. If an offer arrives before anyone has interviewed you, treat it as
a scam.
